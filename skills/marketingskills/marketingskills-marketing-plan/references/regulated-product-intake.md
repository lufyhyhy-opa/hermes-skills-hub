# Regulated and reward-bearing product intake

Use when acquisition or monetization depends on game rewards, paid entry, virtual currency, cash-out, financial value or another regulated product mechanism. This is a scoping and evidence workflow, not a legal opinion or permission to promote restricted activity.

## 1. Resolve the actual mechanism first

Ask the minimum decision-changing questions in selectable cards when preferred:

- Who pays, in which market, and what age/eligibility group is served?
- What does payment buy: a fixed access/content entitlement, a contest entry, or value exposed to a win/loss outcome?
- Can rewards be redeemed for money, vouchers, goods or other external value, or transferred/traded directly or indirectly?
- Is the outcome determined by skill, chance or both?
- Which operating entity, release/authorization evidence and legal review cover the actual flow?

Keep overlapping reward capabilities multi-select. Record skipped items as unknown; neither a recommendation label nor a timeout establishes a product fact. Treat words such as entertainment, coins and points as labels until the underlying flows are explained. Adult targeting alone does not establish legal clearance.

## 2. Select the permissible work scope

- If the described flow is wagering with real-value stakes or rewards, do not build recruitment, deposit-conversion, betting engagement or promotional automation for it. Offer non-promotional compliance preparation or a genuinely non-wagering redesign.
- If the user selects a non-wagering redesign, describe exactly what a buyer receives independent of the result. Record cash-out, external-value exchange, stake loss, transferable value, pay-to-win and paid chance-based reward mechanics as excluded scope where applicable.
- Keep commercial hypotheses tentative: a proposed access pass, trial length or price is not an approved SKU, implemented entitlement or proof of demand. When the user later says price is undecided or not locked, clear those illustrative amounts from the active decision pack so they cannot ride forward as selected pricing.
- Verify applicable claims against current primary sources and have a qualified local professional review the real product. Do not present removal of one feature as a blanket legal conclusion.

## 3. Define release readiness separately from content gates

Create a product/release prerequisite, named distinctly from plan and publishing approvals. Its evidence should identify:

| Area | Evidence required before public traffic or payment |
|---|---|
| Product | Versioned build and tested paths showing the new mechanics and entitlements |
| Routing | Approved destination URLs with legacy wagering routes excluded |
| Legal and policies | Review of applicable release/operating requirements, payments, consumer terms, data and eligibility |
| Existing users | Reconciled legacy balances and obligations, with an approved migration/remedy plan |
| Operations | Payment-to-entitlement tests, support ownership, consent and incident controls |

Do not erase, confiscate or silently convert old balances. Do not use a redesign announcement to send users into unchanged legacy mechanics. Preserve old evidence and make the current proposal visibly separate.

Plan approval may authorize the next permitted production stage; it does not substitute for release readiness or final publication approval. Likewise, approval to investigate a redesign does not authorize editing the live game, charging customers or connecting accounts. Avoid circular dependencies: a plan for a public experiment cannot require that experiment's completed results as its own approval prerequisite.

## 4. Hand off a bounded proposal

Provide confirmed scope, excluded mechanics, unresolved evidence, responsible roles, conditional roadmap and exact approvals needed. Keep hypotheses and current live behavior in separate fields. Scope research to what changes the next decision; do not let broad legal or platform research indefinitely replace the requested decision-ready plan.
