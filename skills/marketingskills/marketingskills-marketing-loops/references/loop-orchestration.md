# Loop Orchestration & Rollout

Loops aren't independent scripts — they compose into a marketing operating system. This reference covers how they fit together and the order to adopt them so you never build them all at once.

## The system view

Loops fall into four layers. Data flows down and learnings flow back up.

```
SENSING        analytics-anomaly · tracking-QA · weekly-marketing-review
                  │  (detect what changed; trust the numbers first)
                  ▼
DIAGNOSTIC     per-stage watchers — onboarding drop-off, churn-signal,
               ranking-drop, landing-page regression, ad-fatigue, …
                  │  (figure out what to do about it)
                  ▼
ACTION         staged drafts, nudges, outreach, budget moves
                  │  (mostly human-checkpointed)
                  ▼
LEARNING       experiment-backlog · campaign-postmortem · voice-of-customer
                  │  (capture what worked)
                  └──────────────► feeds back into SENSING & DIAGNOSTIC
```

Key connective tissue:
- **weekly-marketing-review is the router.** It reads top-line metrics and dispatches each notable mover to the loop that owns it. It's the one loop that sees the whole board.
- **tracking-QA + analytics-anomaly are the foundation.** Every other loop reads from analytics. If tracking is broken, every downstream loop acts on lies. These come first.
- **experiment-backlog is the sink.** Hypotheses generated by many loops (signup-leak, pricing, onboarding, voice-of-customer) converge here, then hand off to `ab-testing`. Don't let each loop run its own tests.
- **voice-of-customer is a source.** Customer language it mines feeds copy for ad-fatigue, lifecycle-email, landing-page, and pricing loops.
- **campaign-postmortem closes the loop.** Its learnings become next quarter's hypotheses and plan inputs, and the ones that hold are proposed for the **Learnings** table in the product-marketing context doc, where every drafting skill reads them.

Avoid duplicate ownership: when two loops could act on the same signal, one owns the action and the other just flags. (E.g., an at-risk account belongs to churn-signal, not expansion/upsell — never upsell an account that's churning.)

## Continuous-content rollout for a marketing-only brief

Use this branch when the requested outcome is multichannel reach and voluntary contact acquisition. Keep payment recovery, pricing and revenue expansion outside this branch; those loops belong to a broader business assignment.

1. **Fix the brief and accounting units.** Record the audience, confirmed product facts, source rights, channel roles, CTA, contact benefit, budget period and package ceiling. Carry one shared contract into strategy and design handoffs. Keep the contact North Star separate from platform views and the business North Star.
2. **Build the capture and measurement foundation.** Stage the landing/demo route, optional contact path, consent proof, CRM dedupe/suppression, content-source tags and weekly report. Budget backend/CRM/SEO setup separately or reduce content scope; a strategy website is not an operational acquisition form.
3. **Define one complete package.** Specify source footage, script/copy, required graphic and text variants, final video, thumbnail variants, QA coverage and the approved destinations. Record which prepared variants will actually be distributed; prepared drafts are not publications. Preserve the applicable human gates and repair limits.
4. **Calculate feasible cadence by phase.** For each modeled period, declare setup hours/cash, total packages `P`, package labor `P × per-package labor`, service, approval, reporting and orchestration. Include the first seed once in its period's ceiling; retain unique package IDs in later periods rather than recreating or adding another seed. Separate a lower-output setup period from steady state and fund each additional cycle explicitly. Count placements as eligible asset-to-destination assignments; a multi-image post is one placement. Reconcile the creative inventory, channel calendar, complete QA load and hours in code; label a four-week capacity window separately from a calendar month.
5. **Exercise one canary.** After the required authority exists, complete source → brief → draft → real media → repurpose → QA → human approval → one native/official dispatch → independent readback. Measure actual work and rework. Add destinations one at a time with their own permission, approval and observed-result evidence.
6. **Run a bounded batch rhythm.** Collect sources and questions, prepare briefs, write/edit, review, schedule, serve responses and learn on assigned days or slots. Replenish evergreen material and an approved queue after repeatability is demonstrated; do not spend the entire package ceiling filling a buffer before testing the first package. Missing source, exhausted QA corrections or excess review load holds the dependent work instead of forcing the calendar.
7. **Automate in four separable loops.** Use intake to stage sourced ideas, repurposing to stage complete packages, publishing to act only on approved slots, and weekly learning to propose the next backlog. Fill all nine anatomy fields from SKILL.md for each loop. Assign one action owner for shared signals; keep loop outputs inactive until their authorized rollout. In plan/demo scope, implement an offline simulator first: terminals limited to HOLD / RECONCILE / SKIP_DUPLICATE / SUPPRESS / HALT / READY_FOR_MANUAL_*; `side_effect_performed` always false; no PUBLISHED/SENT/SPENT and no network.
8. **Choose the next capacity level from evidence.** Compare qualified contacts, closed-cohort engagement, actual hours, rework and cash for the same period. Propose one additional channel or a higher package rate with a new capacity allocation; a second completed cycle indicates operational learning, not statistical proof of channel lift. A capacity lab may accept role-hour inputs for fit/shortfall math, but blank fields stay UNKNOWN and the lab must not flip live-ready until named non-overlap assignment exists.
9. **When upgrading an existing WBS into an operator pack**, freeze the task register and add channel playbooks, research-only briefs, capped placement board, inactive contracts, sequential experiments and null weekly-report defaults — see `marketing-plan` → `references/operator-pack.md`. Do not reopen strategy or invent new package IDs inside the board.

At dispatch, make each specialist's ownership concrete: strategy owns the canonical plan and task table, design owns the presentation, and the parent owns integration and deployment. The parent reconciles their dates, units, metrics and download files before public handoff; parallel drafting must not create competing default plans. For long execution-upgrade plans, keep the budget model and full task CSV generation with the parent, and treat stalled or empty child summaries as incomplete until files are verified on disk.

## Rollout path (adopt applicable stages in this order)

Select stages from the current outcome and business model. For a full revenue operating system, use the sequence below; for marketing-only contact acquisition, use the branch above. Add a loop only after its dependencies are proven and a human can use its output.

**Stage 0 — Foundation (trust the data + see the board).**
`tracking-QA`, `weekly-marketing-review`.
You cannot run any loop responsibly on untrustworthy data or without a full-funnel pulse. This is non-negotiable and comes first.

**Stage 1 — Plug the leaks (highest ROI, protects existing revenue).**
`failed-payment/dunning`, `churn-signal`, `lifecycle-email-refresh`.
Recovering customers you already have is cheaper than acquiring new ones. Dunning alone often pays for the whole system.

**Stage 2 — Convert what you already get (fix the bucket before adding water).**
`onboarding drop-off`, `signup-funnel-leak`, `trial-conversion`.
More traffic into a leaky funnel is waste. Seal activation and conversion next.

**Stage 3 — Grow the top (now scale acquisition).**
`keyword-gap`, `content-repurposing`, `ad-fatigue`, `social-listening`, `analytics-anomaly`.
With the bucket sealed, turn on demand generation and the safety-net anomaly watcher.

**Stage 4 — Optimize monetization.**
`pricing-page-experiment`, `paywall-optimization`, `PQL/upgrade-intent`, `expansion/upsell`.
Once volume is healthy, tune revenue per user — judged on revenue quality, not conversion alone.

**Stage 5 — Compounding & advocacy.**
`referral-nudge`, `review-and-UGC-harvest`, `review-site-management`, `case-study-sourcing`, `partner-pipeline`, `brand-mention/reputation`, `experiment-backlog`, `campaign-postmortem`.
The flywheel: happy customers and earned media that feed back into acquisition, plus the learning loops that make everything compound.

The remaining catalog loops (content-decay, striking-distance push, AI-answer check, AI-answer accuracy audit, claim-drift, signal-sweep, reply-triage, cold-domain health, sequence-retro, internal-linking, programmatic-SEO quality, content-calendar refill, weekly ads-check, paid-search query-mining, retargeting-hygiene, landing-page regression, community-engagement, competitor-watch, backlink-prospecting, directory-submission, feature-adoption, lead-capture-asset, email-deliverability, voice-of-customer, agent-default check, niche-outlier, format-finding, creator-scouting, launch-reaction) slot into the stage that matches their function as each channel becomes a priority.

## Rollout rules

- **One at a time.** Prove a loop earns its keep (someone acts on its output, it moves its metric) before adding the next.
- **Foundation before growth.** Establish trustworthy tracking, the agreed conversion/capture path and a feedback owner before increasing acquisition; irrelevant revenue loops are not prerequisites for a marketing-only brief.
- **Cap the total.** If you're running more loops than you can review the output of, you have vanity loops. Retire the ones nobody acts on.
- **Re-audit quarterly.** Recalibrate thresholds, kill dead loops, promote the ones that consistently drive action.

For worked examples of several loops composed into one channel operator, see `seo-operator.md` and `outbound-operator.md`.
